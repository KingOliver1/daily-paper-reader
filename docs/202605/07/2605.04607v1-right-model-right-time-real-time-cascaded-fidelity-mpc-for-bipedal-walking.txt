# Right Model, Right Time: Real-Time Cascaded-Fidelity MPC for Bipedal Walking 

Franek Stark 1, Felix Wiebe 1,2, Shubham Vyas 1,2, Dennis Mronga 1, Frank Kirchner 1,2

Abstract —This paper presents a multi-phase whole-body model predictive control approach for bipedal walking, combining a detailed whole-body model in the near horizon with a simplified single-rigid-body model in the later prediction steps. This reduces computational complexity while retaining prediction capabilities. The resulting nonlinear optimal control problem is solved using sequential quadratic programming (SQP) in acados. Using a prior specified contact schedule and a target walking speed, the controller optimizes joint torques without depending on prior selected foot step locations. The controller is validated in MuJoCo simulation on the 18-DoF bipedal robot HyPer-2. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Legged robotics has advanced rapidly in recent years, with dynamic locomotion successfully demonstrated on quadrupeds and, later, on bipedal robots. Thereby, two different control paradigms are prevalent: reinforcement learning (RL) and model predictive control (MPC). While RL has shown impres-sive results and benefits strongly from improved simulations and computing power, it still faces well-known challenges such as time-consuming reward shaping, sample inefficiency, lack of generalization and open questions regarding safety. For this reason, MPC remains attractive, because it allows the controller to exploit system dynamics online and reason about constraints directly. However, for high-dimensional problems, such as bipedal walking in humanoids, the computational complexity of the optimal control problem remains too high, especially when aiming for an online solution. Hence, many works rely on reduced-order models in MPC, in combination with instantaneous Whole-Body Control (WBC), which uses the full dynamics [1], [2]. A common limitation of these approaches is that contact forces and body motions are planned independently. Here, additional heuristics make systematic tuning of the different control levels difficult and the use of simplified models in MPC may lead to dynamically infeasible motion plans. Recently, whole-body MPC for bipedal robots has become feasible due to advances in fast numerical optimal control, in particular through differential dynamic program-ming (DDP) based solvers [3]. Recent studies proposed multi-phase MPC formulations where a detailed model is used in the early steps of the prediction horizon and simpler models as the horizon progresses [4]–[6]. This reduces the problem complexity, while preserving predictive capabilities, which again improves the trade-off between solution quality and computational effort. These methods are available in mature general-purpose software frameworks, such as acados [8], enabling robotics applications to leverage the advances in numerical optimization, without custom solver implementations. In the case of bipedal humanoid walking, this was demonstrated for a whole-body NMPC controller [9]. They use a single SQP iteration per control step in a custom general purpose solver, hence focusing on solution speed over accuracy. Motivated by these developments, this paper presents a whole-body multi-phase MPC for bipedal walking that com-bines several of the above ideas in a single framework. The proposed controller employs a whole-body (WB) model in the early steps of the horizon and a single-rigid-body (SRB) model in the later steps (see Fig. 1) to reduce computational cost, while solving the resulting nonlinear optimal control problem in a real-time SQP setting with only a small num-ber of iterations per control cycle. The entire formulation is implemented using acados [8], a general-purpose open-source framework, which recently introduced direct support for multi-phase optimal control problems [10]. Our controller was successfully tested on the 18 degrees-of-freedom (DoF) bipedal robot HyPer-2 in a MuJoCo simulation using purely torque control and a control frequency of 100 Hz .II. To investigate the relationship between the prediction hori-zon of whole-body and single rigid body model, the ratio α

is varied, while keeping N = 10 fixed. The results are shown in Fig. 3. When using Nwb ≤ 2, that is α ∈ { 0.1, 0.2}, the robot quickly became unstable. When increasing Nwb , both, the height and velocity error reduce, where the former has its minimum at α = 0 .6. At the same time, the solve time increases linearly, such that for α = 0 .6 the target solve time of 10 ms is not met. For α > 0.7, the robot fell. Finally, we vary the discretization step sizes to study the influence of the resulting prediction horizon length, T =

Nwb ∆twb +Nsrb ∆tsrb . Here, ∆twb was varied directly, while 

∆tsrb was computed according to ∆tsrb = 5 ∆ twb . The results are shown in Fig. 4. Unlike in the two previous experiments, the discretization step sizes only have a minor influence on the solve time. The robot is unstable for ∆twb < 15 ms 

(T < 90 ms ). While the velocity error is largely independent of the discretization step size, the height error is smallest at approximately ∆twb = 21 ms (T = 126 ms ). Beyond a step size of 21 ms , the robot exhibits increasing knee buckling and approaches a singular configuration, eventually falling for step sizes above ∆twb > 37 ms (T > 222 ms ). IV. D ISCUSSION AND FUTURE WORK 

The results demonstrate that real-time whole-body MPC with a multi-phase horizon can be achieved using a stan-dard nonlinear MPC framework from the control community, without relying on specialized or problem-specific solvers. Stable walking is obtained with only a small number of SQP iterations, and the reduced model complexity in the further part of the prediction horizon decreases solve time effectively. However, the analysis is not yet conclusive. Varying the solve time in reality degrades control frequency. The horizon com-position via α also changes the effective prediction horizon 

T due to the different discretization step sizes in each phase. As a result, the instabilities we observe for larger values of α

(e.g., α > 0.7) may be also attributed to a shortened horizon. Future work will therefore focus on a more systematic and controlled analysis of the multi-phase design, including independent variation of horizon length, model composition, and discretization. Extending the framework to incorporate additional model abstractions, is another promising direction. Finally, validation on the real HyPer-2 system is required to assess stability in realistic conditions. ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS

B. Solver setup 

The acados SQP solver is configured to use a Gauss-Newton Hessian approximation and the sparsity exploiting interior-point method (IPM) solver HPIPM [14] in BALANCE 

for the quadratic program (QP)-subproblems. Warm-starting is enabled at both the NLP and QP levels, including ini-tialization of the first QP from the previous NLP solution. An adaptive Levenberg-Marquardt regularization is employed to improve numerical robustness, together with projection-based regularization of the QP. A fixed-step globalization strategy is used, avoiding line-search overhead and favor-ing predictable runtimes. The adaptive QP tolerance strategy
